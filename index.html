What Should You Do Immediately After a Crash?

A car crash can happen in seconds, but the steps you take right after it can protect your health, your insurance claim, and your legal rights. This guide explains what to do immediately after a car accident in Las Vegas, how to recognize hidden injuries, and when to get medical help.

Updated for 2026 Car Accident Care Las Vegas Medical + Insurance Steps

After a crash, the most important thing is to stay calm and focus on safety. Even if the collision seems minor, your body may still be injured and symptoms can appear later. In Las Vegas, busy traffic, high speeds, and distracted driving make fast action even more important.

Important: Even if you feel fine, adrenaline can hide pain after a collision. Hidden injuries like whiplash, concussions, soft tissue damage, and internal injuries may not show symptoms right away.

Why Immediate Care Matters

Getting checked early helps protect your health and creates a clear medical record. That record can support your insurance claim and show that your injuries were caused by the crash. Early evaluation also helps prevent complications from worsening over time.

Step-by-Step Actions After a Crash

1. Check for injuries immediately

First, check yourself, your passengers, and anyone else involved. Ask whether anyone is hurt and whether it is safe to move. If someone is seriously injured, call emergency services right away and avoid moving them unless there is an immediate danger such as fire.

2. Call 911

Call 911 if there are injuries, major vehicle damage, blocked traffic, or if anyone is unconscious. Police and paramedics can help at the scene, and the official report can become important later for insurance and documentation. In Nevada, drivers also have a duty to stop, exchange information, and render reasonable aid after a crash.

3. Move to safety if possible

If the vehicles are drivable and it is safe to do so, move them out of traffic and turn on your hazard lights. If the crash is severe, someone is trapped, or the cars are not safe to move, stay where you are and wait for responders. Safety comes before everything else.

4. Document the scene

Take photos of vehicle damage, the roadway, skid marks, traffic signs, weather conditions, and visible injuries. Exchange names, phone numbers, license plate numbers, driver’s license details, and insurance information. If witnesses saw the crash, ask for their contact details too.

5. Get medical attention right away

Visit an urgent care center, emergency room, or accident injury clinic as soon as possible, even if symptoms seem mild. Common diagnostic tools include physical exams, X-rays, MRI scans, CT scans, and neurological checks. Early care is especially important for neck pain, headaches, dizziness, and back pain.

6. Watch for delayed symptoms

Some injuries do not appear until hours or days later. Warning signs include neck stiffness, headaches, dizziness, fatigue, numbness, blurred vision, memory problems, shoulder pain, and worsening back pain. If these symptoms appear, seek medical care immediately.

7. Understand common crash injuries

Whiplash is one of the most common injuries after a rear-end collision. Other frequent problems include back injuries, soft tissue damage, head injuries like concussions, and joint injuries in the shoulders, knees, and wrists. Prompt treatment can reduce the risk of long-term pain and limited movement.

8. Keep insurance and medical records

Save every medical report, prescription, invoice, and follow-up instruction. Keep notes about symptoms, missed work, and appointments. These records help with insurance claims and make it easier to show the full impact of the crash.

9. Follow your treatment plan

Your doctor may recommend rest, pain management, physical therapy, imaging, or chiropractic care depending on your injuries. Follow the plan closely and attend every follow-up visit. Skipping treatment can delay healing and may make symptoms worse.

10. Know when to visit a special accident care clinic

If pain lasts more than 24 to 48 hours, or if stiffness, swelling, dizziness, confusion, or mobility problems get worse, seek specialized care. A dedicated car accident injury clinic in Las Vegas can provide injury-focused evaluation and the paperwork needed for insurance documentation.

Common Hidden Injury Symptoms

Hidden injuries often show up after the adrenaline wears off. That is why many doctors recommend checking for symptoms even when there is no obvious pain at the scene.

  • Neck stiffness or whiplash pain.
  • Headaches or pressure in the head.
  • Dizziness or balance issues.
  • Back pain or reduced movement.
  • Numbness, tingling, or weakness.
  • Blurred vision or light sensitivity.
  • Fatigue, confusion, or memory problems.

Frequently Asked Questions

What should I do immediately after a car accident?

Check for injuries, call 911 if needed, move to safety if possible, document the scene, and get medical attention as soon as you can.

Do I need to see a doctor even if I feel okay?

Yes. Many crash injuries are delayed, and pain may appear hours or days later. A medical exam can catch hidden injuries early and create important documentation.

What injuries are most common after a crash?

Common injuries include whiplash, back pain, concussion, soft tissue injury, shoulder pain, and joint damage.

When should I go to the emergency room?

Go to the ER if you have severe pain, unconsciousness, chest pain, trouble breathing, heavy bleeding, confusion, or numbness and weakness.

Should I take photos after the accident?

Yes. Photos of damage, the road, license plates, and visible injuries can help support your insurance and legal records.

How long can symptoms take to appear?

Symptoms may appear immediately or take several hours to several days to develop, especially with whiplash, concussion, and soft tissue injuries.

Can I move my car after a crash?

If the car is drivable and it is safe to do so, move it out of traffic and turn on hazard lights. If the crash is severe or someone is hurt, wait for emergency responders.

Why is medical documentation important?

Medical records show when you were injured, what symptoms you had, and what treatment you received. This can be very important for insurance claims.

Final Thoughts

A car accident can feel overwhelming, but the right steps can protect your health and your claim. Check for injuries, call for help, document everything, and get medical attention quickly.

If you are dealing with delayed pain, stiffness, headaches, or dizziness after a crash, do not ignore it. Early care is one of the best ways to recover well and avoid long-term problems.
